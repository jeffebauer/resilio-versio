# Starting points

Six classic dub settings to start from, then five characters from dub and dub techno, with tips for getting there. Then move by ear. Found a setting worth sharing? [Suggest it](https://github.com/jeffebauer/resilio-versio/issues/new?template=sound_idea.yml), and it may join this page, with credit. The Versio has no preset memory: set the knobs by hand (in the plugin, type the values or save them as your DAW's presets).

**Reading the panels.** Each starting point shows the panel with its knobs and switches set, and the exact values underneath. Knob values run from 0 (fully left, 7 o'clock) to 1 (fully right, 5 o'clock); noon is 0.5, and each hour is about 0.1. The switches are drawn in their position: up, centre or down.

WOBBLE is bipolar: noon is still, left drifts like tape, right is a vibrato. Every starting point sits just left of noon for a little tape drift. TONE right of noon is the “Big Knob” low cut, so the starting points that sit there (Tubby snare splash, Tight slap) are slightly thinned.

On the hardest drum hits, Tubby snare splash, Drowned howl and Valve tank drop can light the output LEDs red (the limiter catching peaks): that's normal there. Skanks and pads stay clear of it in every starting point.

## Tubby snare splash

**Sounds like:** the snare thrown into the spring: a bright crack, a loose boing, a dripping tail of about 2.5 seconds with tape grit.

**Play into it:** a snare or rimshot, one hit per bar on the backbeat. Best on a send: BLEND fully right on a return, then ride the send.

**Move next:** TENSION further left for a bigger, slower boing. TONE further right to thin it out like Tubby's “Big Knob” (the snare gets a nasal ring). SPLASH past 3 o'clock for a much bigger clang. DRIVE up for dirtier drips. Flip ATTITUDE to VALVE mid-tail for a one-off crash.

## Skank chord wash

**Sounds like:** a smooth, dense halo behind offbeat chords, warm and slightly moving, never smearing the groove.

**Play into it:** a guitar or organ skank, short stabs on the offbeat.

**Move next:** DECAY down if the chords start to blur into each other. WOBBLE further left for a seasick, worn-tape drift (or right of noon for a steady vibrato). TANK to 1 for a sparser, splashier tank. Or TANK to ECHO with TENSION on dotted 1/8 to skank into the tape echo.

## Tight slap

**Sounds like:** a short, pingy spring slap (about half a second): the "amp in a small room" spring, quick repeats, still clearly springy.

**Play into it:** melodica, vocals, rimshots: anything busy that a long tail would swamp.

**Move next:** TENSION a little left if it's too metallic. BLEND up for an obvious slapback. TONE left to sit it behind the source.

## Drowned howl

**Sounds like:** the tank tipping into its own feedback: a rough, moving, siren-like roar that keeps going after the input stops. It never becomes a clean tone.

**Play into it:** one hit or a dub-siren blip, then stop and ride the knobs.

**Move next:** ride TONE and TENSION to steer the pitch and colour of the howl. WOBBLE further left for more seasickness. Pull DECAY below about 4 o'clock to let it fall back into a normal tail (it fades in a second or two). Watch the output LEDs: red here means the limiter is holding it.

## Valve tank drop

**Sounds like:** a hard hit lands like the whole tank was kicked: a big metallic crash that boings out for about 3.5 seconds. One loose spring keeps it sparse and splashy.

**Play into it:** throw the snare or rim on the drop, the one before the bass comes back in: hold the button (or send a gate) for that one hit.

**Move next:** a gate sequence for rhythmic throws. DECAY past 4 o'clock and the crash can build into the howl. ATTITUDE to TAPE or CLEAN for a softer knock.

## Mix-bus spring

**Sounds like:** a light, short, dense spring you feel more than hear, gluing a whole mix together.

**Play into it:** a submix or a full stereo mix.

**Move next:** BLEND a touch up or down until you only notice it when it's off. TONE left if the highs get busy. Keep DRIVE and SPLASH low so peaks stay clean. Pads in the mix are looked after: the tank turns down held sounds before they reach the limiter.

## Dub and dub techno characters

Dub and dub techno use the same tools in different ways. Roots dub throws single hits into the springs and rides the desk. Dub techno, from Basic Channel onwards, turns chord stabs into long, filtered washes and echoes that sit under a steady kick. A few tips, then five settings to start from.

- **Use a send.** Set BLEND fully right on a return channel and send only what you want soaked: the snare, a chord stab, a siren. The dry stays on its own fader.
- **Keep the lows out of the tank.** TONE a little right of noon keeps the reverb out of the kick and bass, which matters most for dub techno's long washes. The Hold (DECAY fully up in CLEAN or TAPE) also dips under the kick and bass it hears.
- **Pick the grit with ATTITUDE.** CLEAN for glassy dub techno beds. TAPE's 12-bit grain for roots dub. VALVE's 10-bit grit and lopsided saturation for rough, broken-amp dub. The grit comes before TONE, so TONE right thins it too.
- **Ride, don't set.** Dub engineers played the desk. TONE, DECAY and the throw are moves to make while the track runs, not settings to leave alone.
- **Clock the echo.** Send the gate input one pulse per quarter note from your sequencer (or tap the button), then pick the division with TENSION: dotted 1/8 sits at about noon, 1/8 at about 1:30, 1/4 at about 10:30.

### Chord stab into the bed

**Sounds like:** dub techno's chord and bed: a short stab blooms into a held, slowly moving wash that sits under the track and dips out of the way of the kick.

**Play into it:** short minor chord stabs, one or two a bar. The bed ducks under the kick and bass that reach the input, so feed it the loop (or a send with the kick in it) for the pumping; a send of the chords alone simply holds.

**Move next:** ATTITUDE to TAPE for a little grain. TONE further right to keep the bed thin and out of the bass. Pull DECAY below about 4 o'clock and the bed fades like a normal tail. WOBBLE a touch further left for a seasick drift.

### Echo chord

**Sounds like:** a chord stab trailing off in dotted-eighth repeats, each one blurring into the springs and getting darker and duller as it goes.

**Play into it:** short chord stabs with the echo clocked from your sequencer (or tapped in on the button).

**Move next:** ride TONE slowly left and right for the filter-swept chord. DECAY up for longer trails; at the very top the repeats hold. TENSION to about 10:30 for quarter notes and a slower pulse.

### Telephone snare

**Sounds like:** the snare's reverb thinned to a nasal, telephone-like ring: the “Big Knob” near its top, where Tubby's filter turned a snare into a squeak.

**Play into it:** a snare or rimshot on a send. Ride TONE between noon and fully right as the tail rings.

**Move next:** move TONE in jumps rather than a smooth sweep, like the original's steps. SPLASH past 3 o'clock for a bigger clang. ATTITUDE to VALVE for coarser grit on the ring.

### Murky tape echo

**Sounds like:** a dark, wobbling tape echo, its repeats smearing and bending: the murk of a worn tape echo into a spring, as in Lee "Scratch" Perry's Space Echo dubs.

**Play into it:** a melodica line, a vocal phrase or a skank. Clocked, TENSION at 1:30 gives eighth notes; unclocked it sets the time freely.

**Move next:** turn TENSION while the repeats play to bend them like a Space Echo's rate knob. WOBBLE further left for more seasick tape. DRIVE up for thicker, more saturated repeats.

### Siren swoop

**Sounds like:** a dub siren blip that keeps repeating, held at the top of DECAY by the tape's saturation, warbling and swooping in pitch as you turn TENSION.

**Play into it:** one short siren or synth blip, then stop and play the knobs.

**Move next:** unclocked, TENSION swoops smoothly; with a clock, each step to a new division swoops like tape. Pull DECAY back and the repeats die away. TONE right for a thin, distant siren.
